Yohann (Para 1) - Digital Transformation refers to the use of digital technologies by banks to improve their services, internal operations and customer interactions to reduce dependency on traditional, manual and expensive banking methods. It involves bank moving key services and internal processes to digital platforms such as mobile banking, internet banking or automated systems. This includes digital payments, online account management services and robotic process automation, which reduces paperwork, staff workload, and reliance on physical branches, especially during periods of economic uncertainty. This leads to lower operating expenses as banks spend less on branch maintenance, paperwork, and staff costs. As a result, efficiency is improved and transactions are processed faster, allowing banks to serve more customers at a lower cost. This helps maintain profitability during periods of challenging economic conditions even when interest income and customer spending may decline. For example, Mauritius Commercial Bank (MCB) launched the MCB Juice, an app allowing customers to carry out transfers, pay bills, and manage accounts without visiting branches. However, applying Digital Transformation requires significant initial investment in technology and staff training, which may temporarily affect short-term profits.

Farhaan (Para 3) - Diversification is essentially a bank’s way of ensuring they aren’t just “one-trick ponies” that rely solely on loan interest to keep the lights on. This strategy involves expanding into non-interest income areas, such as wealth management, insurance (bancassurance) and digital transaction fees, which effectively turns the bank into a kind of financial supermarket. This move becomes especially important when the economy takes a hit, as if loan demand falls or interest rates fluctuate, these extra income streams act as a safety net. They help banks to steady profits and protect themselves from the ups and downs of traditional lending. In Mauritius, this can be seen with MCB, which has developed the Juice app into a lifestyle platform that earns transaction fees, while SBM has integrated bancassurance to offer both life and general insurance to its retail customers. However, although diversification helps to protect profits, it also requires significant investment in staff training, since bankers need to be able to sell these more complex products effectively.


Oman (Para 4) -Strategy, sustainability and adaptability are critical factors to ensure that retail banks remain profitable and relevant in the long run as the economic and financial landscape continues to evolve rapidly in Mauritius. To make sure retail banks remain relevant against non-bank financial players, they must plan to invest heavily in technology such as digital account openings, mobile apps and digital wallets. For example, Absa Bank Mauritius, has launched the Digi-loan and the Digi-account which are cost-effective solutions and helps them deliver more rapidly to customers while remaining profitable. In addition, banks should also consider ESG (Environmental, social and governance) as it is becoming a crucial part of the corporate world and offer products such as green loans. Long term strategy planning of retail banks must also involve training staff and seeking help from fintech companies. These measures will help retail banks remain profitable while supporting economic and financial development.

Ashfarh (Para 5) -Another important step banks should take to remain profitable is prioritising customer needs and improving service quality. This means giving customers personalised services, faster service and reliable support. During an economic crisis, customers become more cautious. They tend to compare banks based on how convenient and smooth the service is. As a result, banks that provide better service are more likely to retain customers and secure their deposits. This also increases profitability through cross-selling products such as loans, insurance, and credit cards. Mauritius has seen banks like MCB and SBM improve customer experience by having dedicated relationship managers, tailored SME banking solutions, and quicker complaint resolution through digital platforms. Examples include SBM which also provides SMEs with specialised desks for loan and business support, while MCB has expanded private banking to offer personalised financial services, like advice on investments. However, maintaining high quality service can increase operating costs, so banks need to find a balance between customer care and efficiency.
