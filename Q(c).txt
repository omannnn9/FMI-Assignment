What is expected and how it will be marked?

For a 25-mark question, examiners expect a critical discussion, not just a description. This means you must identify the main challenges faced by retail banks due to COVID-19, explain how these challenges affect bank profitability and stability, and briefly evaluate their long-term impact. Marks are awarded for clear structure, logical analysis, real-world application (especially Mauritius examples), and evidence of evaluation. Simply listing challenges will not score high marks.

How a 25-mark answer is usually structured

📌 Introduction (2–3 marks)  - (120-150 words)

Brief context of the economic fallout caused by COVID-19

Highlight why retail banks were heavily affected

State that the answer will assess key challenges using relevant examples

📌 Main body (18–20 marks) - 5 paragraphs (120-150 words each)

Split into 4–5 main challenges, each worth about 4–5 marks

For each challenge:

Define the challenge briefly

Explain how COVID-19 caused or worsened it

Analyse the impact on profitability and stability

Apply real examples from Mauritius banks

Evaluate limitations or trade-offs (1 sentence)

📌 Conclusion (2–3 marks) - (80-125 words)

Summarise the main challenges discussed

Emphasise long-term sustainability and profitability

Link banks’ resilience to economic recovery

What to include in your answer (COVID-19 challenges – Retail Banks)

Use this structure for each challenge you discuss (e.g. credit risk, profitability, digital disruption, liquidity, customer confidence).

✅ 1. DEFINE

What it means:
Briefly define the challenge faced by retail banks due to COVID-19.

Rules:

1–2 sentences only

Simple and clear

No long textbook definitions

Example:
Credit risk refers to the possibility that borrowers may be unable to repay their loans on time.

✅ 2. EXPLAIN

What it means:
Explain how COVID-19 caused or worsened this challenge for retail banks.

Rules:

Main part of the paragraph

Link directly to lockdowns, income loss, business closures, uncertainty

Focus on retail banks (households, SMEs)

Example:
During the COVID-19 pandemic, job losses and reduced business activity affected borrowers’ ability to meet loan repayments.

✅ 3. ANALYSE

What it means:
Show cause and effect and link the challenge to bank performance.

Rules:

Use phrases like “This leads to…”, “As a result…”

Clearly link to profitability, stability, or capital

Example:
As a result, banks faced higher non-performing loans and had to increase provisions, which reduced profitability.

✅ 4. APPLY

What it means:
Support your point with real examples from Mauritius.

Rules:

Compulsory for high marks

Mention actual banks and actions taken

Example:
In Mauritius, banks such as MCB and SBM were exposed to higher credit risk in sectors like tourism and SMEs.

🔍 EVALUATE (for higher marks)

What it means:
Briefly assess strengths, weaknesses, or limitations.

Rules:

1 sentence only

Place at the end of the paragraph

Use words like “However”, “Despite this”

Example:
However, loan moratoria reduced short-term defaults but increased uncertainty about future loan performance.

1. Deterioration in Asset Quality (Credit Risk & NPLs)

What to say:

Loan defaults surged among households and SMEs due to job losses and business closures

Banks faced higher credit risk, requiring larger loan-loss provisions

This impacted profitability and weakened balance-sheet strength

Mauritius examples:

MCB and SBM exposed to tourism and SME sectors

Use of stricter credit assessments, early warning systems, and loan restructuring

Link to profit:

Higher NPLs reduce earnings and capital adequacy

Effective monitoring protects long-term financial stability

2. Net Interest Margin (NIM) Compression & Profitability Pressure

What to say:

Ultra-low interest rates reduced lending profitability

Decline in loan demand and slower economic activity weakened traditional retail banking income

Increased operational and provisioning costs added pressure

Mauritius examples:

Reduced consumer and business lending during lockdown periods

Banks relied more on transaction fees and bancassurance for revenue

Link to profit:

Shrinking interest income reduces overall profitability

Diversified income streams help banks remain resilient

3. Operational & Technological Disruptions (Digital Transformation & Remote Work)

What to say:

Branch closures and remote working created operational strain

Rapid digital adoption was necessary to maintain service continuity

Increased cybersecurity risks, system outages, and short-term tech costs

Mauritius examples:

MCB Juice App for payments, transfers, and account management

SBM Internet Banking and digital onboarding platforms

Link to profit:

Digital platforms reduce operating costs long-term

Improve customer experience and retention, but require initial investment

4. Liquidity & Funding Pressures

What to say:

Customers drew down deposits and credit lines due to uncertainty

Banks had to maintain higher liquidity buffers, increasing funding costs

Cash flow management became critical to avoid short-term crises

Mauritius examples:

Banks adjusted repayment schedules and closely monitored cash flows

Bank of Mauritius provided liquidity support measures

Link to profit:

Ensures financial stability and continuity of lending

Protects profitability, but maintaining buffers can temporarily reduce returns

5. Regulatory & Compliance Burdens & Customer Expectations

What to say:

New rules on loan moratoria, capital requirements, and stress testing increased compliance demands

Banks needed to maintain customer trust and deliver quality service amid operational constraints

Balancing regulatory compliance and customer satisfaction was essential

Mauritius examples:

SBM set up dedicated SME desks for loans and business support

MCB enhanced private banking and customer communication during the pandemic

Link to profit:

Maintaining compliance avoids penalties and reputational risk

High-quality service retains deposits and supports cross-selling opportunities
